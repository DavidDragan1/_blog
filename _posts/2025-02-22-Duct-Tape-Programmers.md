>Duct tape programmers are pragmatic. Zawinski popularized Richard Gabriel’s precept of [Worse is Better](http://www.jwz.org/doc/worse-is-better.html). A 50%-good solution that people actually have solves more problems and survives longer than a 99% solution that nobody has because it’s in your lab where you’re endlessly polishing the damn thing. Shipping is a feature. A really important feature. Your product must have it.

Excerpt from [Joel on Software](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2009/09/23/the-duct-tape-programmer/)

I've been slowly realising this perspective myself. As a pragmatist and frankly bit of a pedant, I find myself repeatedly polishing a project to the point either:
1. It's no longer needed/exciting/interesting - I've missed the window of opportunity and the project gets added to the half-finished pile.
2. I know the in and outs of the project to such a depth that I start doubting its efficacy and utility; all the little quirks start to bother me and I resolve to *keep* polishing.

Which way I go is irrelevant because by that point its too late anyway. Instead of the perfect solution I've been toiling away at the target end users get... nothing. The 50% MVP would at least solve *half* of the problem, so it's *100% better*. 

Once the MVP begins to gain traction (simultaneously validating the idea's utility), more features can be incrementally rolled out *in response* to users' reception of the beta.

The 50% approach is not only as I've proven 100% more efficient, but inherently Agile. Agile to the bone. In fact, the very premise of RAD was quick, incremental delivery. Even if time and money weren't factors, *requirements change*.

This may seem entirely obvious to pretty much everyone, ever, but it is one thing to know the logical course of action and entirely another to apply it. 

As always, such is the human condition.
