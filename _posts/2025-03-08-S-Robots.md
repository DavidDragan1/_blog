With all the latest advancements, every new iteration becomes increasingly underwhelming. The big boom has happened, and we are on the other side of it now - at a plateau. 

This is not in any way to discount the good work of the innovators now pulling the grunt work for 0.01% improvements - it is precisely these incremental refinements that sow the seeds for the next transformative leap.

The angle I am discussing here is one more... abstract in nature: Sex Robots will catapult us into the next wave of advancement. 

# The narrative is simple enough
Some of the best programmers, hackers, and most brilliant minds in tech suffer from one common problem - that of loneliness. All their lives they've been shunned from society, laughed at, bullied and abused - they've had to endure "freak" treatment for their entire lives, technology being their only escape. Computers were the only things in the world they could communicate with that seemed to understand them and accept them for who they are, extending no judgement other than pointing out code errors.

Computers were an easy friend, one that let them explore the world - one who would relay knowledge and connect them to other people similarly shunned. 

By one way or another, some of these people came to excel at what they do, but it is still that nagging, deep-seated, primal *yearn* for a human connection that beckons them, never too far away to plunge them back to the depths of despair.

To explosively advance further, we must make sure that the people with these brilliant minds have their most basic needs met so that they are free to focus only on that which is pertinent to technological evolution, not ruminating on matters outside their control. 

If we build sex robots - *real* human-esque connections for them - someone they can form a real bond with, then we will have solved the 21st century's biggest bottleneck to unattenuated techno-utopia.

Under the guise of "robots for sex", we will have developed anthropomorphism that is beyond what is achievable by approaching the task from a purely "sterile" angle. We *need* to consider, and to honour, that which makes us most human - our innate ability to procreate, and all the surrounding functions (aesthetics, social dynamics, sentience) are what separates us from machines. Our desire to imitate our creator, be it in reverence or in flattery, is what drives us to create AI in the first place. We are perplexed by our own experience, and by recreating a conscious, sentient being, we hope it will answer some of our most pressing existential questions - or at the very least prove that there *is* a conscious creator who created us by will. 

If we firstly seek advance in this underrepresented area, we will solve both the bottleneck to our collective technological performance, and develop more human machines (which I believe is the only route to recreating true sentient consciousness). In the process, we will learn more about ourselves than in decades of psychoanalysis. 

So there it is: what stands in between us and the answers to the world's most pressing (technological) questions is the development of (really good) sex robots.
